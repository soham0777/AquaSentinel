The idea

So we put a float under the bridge.

The full-size design, AquaSentinel R3, rendered from our CAD. It has not been built.

It rides the river

A float on a 2-inch pole clamped to a bridge pier. Eight rollers. The river lifts it through 2 m. No motor, no anchor.

It reads the water

Every 15 minutes: pH, dissolved solids, turbidity, temperature. It learns what normal is, and notices when that changes.

It keeps the evidence

The light turns red. An SMS goes out. A pump fills a bottle with that water, at that minute, for the lab.

It names the kind of change. It does not name the chemical. The bottle does that.

Electronics

We drew the board, then milled it ourselves.

Sensor Hub v1.2, 104 × 72 mm, single-sided. KiCad render.

In October the Pi read every sensor itself, through a tray of ten small boards. SOLIDWORKS found two clashes in that tray before we printed anything. So we drew one board instead, and cut it on a desktop CNC on 12 November.

Windows blocked the usual milling program, so we wrote our own small sender. And the pH and TDS boards disturb each other in the same water, so the TDS board is powered only while it is read.

Wired in the tray: Raspberry Pi left, our board with the Arduino Nano in the middle. Photo.

13 November, on the bench: all seven sensors answered through this board, and the light and the pump ran from it.

The rules

  • — It learns the water first: 20 seconds here, 7 days planned in a river.
  • — Hard limits: pH below 6 or above 9 (once pH is calibrated), dissolved solids above 1,400 ppm.
  • — A sudden jump counts even below the limit.
  • — Two unusual readings out of three make an event. One bad reading never does.
  • — A missing or impossible reading is SENSOR FAULT, never "normal".
  • — After an event, four clean readings clear it.

Why we quote 2.66, not 4.4. The study holds the pole rigidly at both ends. Bolted clamps are not rigid, so we also worked it by hand with pinned ends and the carriage load: 64.0 MPa, a factor of 2.66. We quote the lower one.

Who pays

Who it is for, and what it costs

Three people, one moment each

  • — The water-works operator at the town intake, who needs to know in time to close it.
  • — The pollution board officer, who needs water taken at the right minute, not next month.
  • — The people at the ghat, who need a light they can read from the bank, without a phone.

What exists today

CPCB real-time station17 parameters, ₹35–40 lakh each
