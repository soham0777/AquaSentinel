AquaSentinel

At two in the morning a spill looks like water. Nobody is there to take the sample. So we built a float that is.

Our printed 1:5 float on its stand. Photo. The full-size float has not been built.

What it does

It rides a pole clamped to a bridge pier and rises and falls with the river. No motor, no anchor. Every 15 minutes it reads pH, dissolved solids, turbidity and temperature. When the water changes, the light turns red, an SMS goes out, and a pump fills a bottle with that water for the lab.

It names the kind of change: industrial, acidic, runoff, solvent. It does not name the chemical. The bottle does that.

What we actually built

  • — A 1:5 model, printed in 37 parts.
  • — Seven real, full-size sensors on it.
  • — Our own circuit board, drawn in KiCad and milled on a desktop CNC on 12 November.
  • — The code that decides, on a Raspberry Pi.
  • — The full-size design: 66 parts in SOLIDWORKS, six drawing sheets, two stress studies.

What we have not done

  • — Built the full-size float.
  • — Put anything in a river.
  • — Measured how often it raises a false alarm.
  • — Got supplier quotes. The cost is our estimate.

What comes next

A tank test of a full-size float on a 2-inch pole. Then, with permits, 30 days on the Godavari at Kopargaon, with every alert checked against a lab sample.
