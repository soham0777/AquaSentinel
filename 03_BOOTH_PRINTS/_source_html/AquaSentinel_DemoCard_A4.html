Pour something in

The demo, in the order we run it. Clean water goes back in the dish between every step, and we wait for NORMAL before the next one.

#Do thisWait forThen say
1Clean tap water in the dish.NORMAL · greenThis is the river on an ordinary day.
2One spoon of salt. Stir.INDUSTRIAL · red · pump runsDissolved solids jumped. That is what a salty or industrial discharge looks like.
3A little vinegar.ACIDIC · red · pump runsThe pH fell. Something acid is in the water.
4One spoon of soil.RUNOFF · red · pump runsCloudy water, but the salts did not move. That is soil washing in.
5Sanitiser on a tissue, held at the bottle cone.SOLVENT · red · pump runsVapour above the sample. A solvent or a fuel.
6Pull out the temperature probe.SENSOR FAULT · blinking redA broken sensor is a fault. It never says the water is fine.

Before the first visitor

  • Power on. The light is blue for 20 seconds while it learns the water in the dish. Do not demo during blue.
  • The MQ-3 needs to warm up. The software blocks SOLVENT for the first 90 seconds; give it 3 minutes to be safe. Same again after any restart.
  • pH electrode cap off, bulb wet. It must never dry out.
  • Turbidity slot clean. Bottle station dry before each sanitiser run.
  • Measuring spoons, salt, vinegar, soil, sanitiser, tissues and a jug of clean water within reach.

Things to say, because they are true

It tells you what kind of change it saw. It does not tell you which chemical. That is what the bottle is for.

The model proves the chain from a sensor to an alarm. It does not prove the river. A dish is not a river.

One SMS in ten minutes, on purpose, so a phone is not flooded. Do not promise an SMS on every step.

When something goes wrong

The SMS does not arriveShow the event on the screen. Say it did not send. Do not pretend.
A sensor diesThat is step 6. Show the fault. It is the behaviour we designed.
The Nano or the board diesRun the simulator on the Pi, and say out loud that it is the simulator. Show the phone video of the real run.
No screen for the dashboardThe light and the pump still show the whole chain. Use the printed dashboard stills.

Hard questions

Answer the question that was asked. If we have not measured it, say so, and say how we would. Tick the two boxes after the bench test, in pen.

Have you built it?The 1:5 model, yes: printed from our CAD, with the real sensors, our own board and a Raspberry Pi. The full-size float, no. That one is CAD, drawings and calculations.
Is the SMS real?Yes. Sent on , here is the message.
Not yet. The modem is wired; no message has left the board.
Does the Pi really read the board?Yes. It ran for minutes on .
Not proven end to end yet. Each half works on its own.
How do you know it is not just salt?We do not. It says dissolved solids jumped, which is what salt or effluent does. The bottle goes to a lab to name it.
What about false alarms?It wants two unusual readings out of three, and it learns the water first. Real false-alarm rates need weeks in a river. We have none.
Why not buy a sonde?A sonde is a better instrument. It also needs a mount, a logger and a modem, and it keeps no sample. Ours is an early warning that keeps the evidence, for about ₹48,000. That figure is an estimate.
What is simulated?The 30-day test, 4 spills out of 4: synthetic data. The software self-test: simulated readings. The two stress studies: static, on CAD. The full-size float: never built.
2.66 or 4.4 for the pole?Both. 4.4 is the SOLIDWORKS study with both ends fixed: 38.3 MPa against 170 MPa yield. 2.66 is our hand calculation with pinned ends and the carriage load: 64.0 MPa. We quote the lower one.
Is the R3 native SOLIDWORKS?The 66 parts of the R3 are imported bodies. We assembled, mated and simulated them in SOLIDWORKS. The 17 new parts of the 1:5 model are native SOLIDWORKS parts.
Does it learn a baseline for every hour?Our October firmware did. The code on this table keeps one baseline for pH and one for dissolved solids, learned in the first 20 seconds.
What does the ₹48,000 stand on?₹47,886 before GST, at 100 units, from Indian supplier listings in November 2026. Not quotes. The first one costs ₹64,616.
Is it really solar?The design is: about 18 Wh a day against a 20 W panel, on paper. Nothing measured. The model on this table runs from a wall socket.
Is the bottle legal evidence?No. The Water Act has its own sampling procedure. Ours is a triage sample: it tells the Board when and where to take theirs.
What if the river rises more than 2 m?The float is held under at the top stop. That is an open risk. It needs gauge data for the site before any pilot.
