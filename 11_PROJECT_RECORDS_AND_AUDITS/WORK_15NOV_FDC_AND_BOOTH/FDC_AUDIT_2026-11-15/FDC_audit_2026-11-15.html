AquaSentinel · Team Sanjivani Hero's Journey · AG26-1500$41 · audit of the live 3DEXPERIENCE post, 15 November 2026 · checked against the project folder, the 14 November backup on E:, the 5 October FDC deck and the competition emails

Verdict. The post is now honest, specific and readable, which most of the other nine will not be. Its weakness is not the writing any more. It is three things a design jury looks for and cannot find on the page: proof that a real user was asked anything, proof that the hardware chain works end to end, and an explanation of why the November build quietly reverses a decision the October submission was graded on. The first two need your team, not me. The third I have fixed in the text, along with six smaller gaps. Those fixes are now live on the post, together with the corrected finale deck and the current electronics manual (published and checked after a reload on 15 November).

1. The sixty-second test

A juror at a booth on 19 November, or scrolling posts before the pitch, gives a page about a minute. On this post that minute now buys: the river at two in the morning, the scale of the problem in one sourced sentence, what the float does, a photograph of the printed model, and the six-water demo table. That is a good first minute.

What the minute does not yet buy is a reason to believe the electronics work, because every proof on the page is a design document, a render, a simulator screenshot or a team report. The one photograph of the electronics shows garbled printing on the boards (section 3, finding C4). One honest bench video would change the page more than anything else on this list.

3. Does it read like people wrote it?

Mostly, yes. The page now opens on a place and a time, uses objects before numbers, admits two mistakes and a reversal, and ends on what the team does not know. None of the words in your voice guide's banned list appear (checked by search).

Three things still give away a machine hand, and only your team can fix them:

  1. No names inside the story. Nobody on the page did anything. One clause each would do it: who milled the board, who wrote the rules, who printed the 37 parts. I have not added any, because I do not know who did what.
  2. No moment. The 13 November bench day is reported, not remembered. One sentence in your own words, such as what happened the first time the light turned red, is the most human line the page could carry.
  3. Even polish. Every caption is careful. Real teams have one slightly rough photo caption. Let the photos you take on the bench carry that.
