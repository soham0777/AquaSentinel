The system in one picture

Sensor hub. The Nano on our PCB reads the 7 sensors once a second, sequences the TDS power (so it never disturbs the pH electrode), drives the RGB beacon and the pump, and prints one JSON line per second on its USB serial port at 115200.
The Pi. 14_RASPBERRY_PI_SOFTWARE reads the Nano over USB, converts volts to units, decides (NORMAL / ACIDIC / INDUSTRIAL / RUNOFF / SOLVENT / SENSOR FAULT), shows a full-screen dashboard on port 8080, writes daily CSV logs and sends one SMS per 10 minutes through the SIM800L.
Power. One AUX 5 V charger feeds J12. The step-up makes 9 V for the pH board; the step-down makes 4.0 V for the SIM800L. The Nano is powered by the Pi's USB cable.

Bench build 1 — power first

  1. Nothing connected yet. Plug the AUX 5 V charger into the wall; connect its + and − to the J12 screw terminal.
  2. Step-up (J14): connect IN+/IN− to the 5 V rail and, with a multimeter, set its output to 9.0 V.
  3. Step-down (J16): feed its input from the 9 V rail and set its output to 4.0 V.
  4. Measure first: 5 V on the sensor headers (J1–J5), 9 V on J15, 4.0 V on J17. Only then connect the pH board, the SIM800L and the sensors.
  5. Pump driver: J13 (+/−) powers the Mini L298N.
Power-up voltages to check on the board
Common mistakes: using the old 12 V adapter (the board wants 5 V on J12) · powering the SIM800L from 5 V (it takes 4.0 V) · powering the pH board from 5 V (it takes 9 V).

Bench build 2 — the board itself

The board was designed in KiCad and milled in-house on a desktop CNC (TwoTrees TTC-450 Pro, 12 Nov 2026): isolation, spot drill, outline score, then 103 × 1.0 mm holes + 6 pilots. KiCad DRC: 0 unconnected nets, 0 clearance errors.

Build steps
  1. Socket the Nano (USB port to the left).
  2. Solder the headers J1–J18 and the few on-board parts: R1 4.7 kΩ (DS18B20 pull-up), R2–R4 220 Ω (beacon), R7 10 kΩ (pump pull-down), R8 4.7 kΩ (pH signal), C2 1000 µF (SIM800L), the 4.7 k/10 k divider on J18.
  3. Beep-test every track against the copper-colour map before powering anything.
Re-milling: full repeatable procedure and every command file in PCB_SENSOR_HUB_v1/PCB_MILL_AGAIN_step_by_step.pdf and design/gcode/. The Nano is not soldered — it sits in the socket.

SIM800L and the link to the Pi

Power (J17)

  • VCC = 4.0 V from the step-down (1000 µF C2 on the board — the module needs it for TX bursts).
  • RXD, TXD, GND on J17; screw the SMA antenna on.
  • Insert the SIM (no PIN), with SMS balance.

UART to the Pi (J18)

  • GND → Pi pin 6.
  • Pi TX pin 8 → on-board 4.7 k/10 k divider → SIM RXD (5 V → 3.3 V logic).
  • SIM TXD → Pi RX pin 10.
  • The Pi talks at 9600 baud on /dev/serial0 (GPIO14/15).

Flash the Arduino Nano

  1. Arduino IDE → Tools → Manage Libraries → install OneWire, DallasTemperature and DHT sensor library (Adafruit).
  2. Board: Arduino Nano; Processor: ATmega328P (Old Bootloader) for most clones (try ATmega328P if the upload fails); Port: the Nano's COM port.
  3. Open PCB_SENSOR_HUB_v1/firmware/aquasentinel_nano/aquasentinel_nano.ino.
  4. Confirm #define BOARD_PCB 1 (this manual's wiring). Upload.
  5. Serial Monitor at 115200: you should see one JSON line per second.

Set up the Raspberry Pi

The software lives in 14_RASPBERRY_PI_SOFTWARE/aquasentinel. Internet is needed only for the setup.

  1. Copy the folder to the Pi: ~/aquasentinel.
  2. Run bash install.sh. It does everything: installs python3-serial, Chromium and unclutter · enables the serial port for the SIM800L (raspi-config + enable_uart=1 + dtoverlay=disable-bt, no login shell) · installs the aquasentinel systemd service (auto-start, auto-restart) · sets the dashboard to open full-screen at boot.
  3. Put the phone numbers in config.json: "sms_numbers": ["+91XXXXXXXXXX"].
  4. sudo reboot — the dashboard opens by itself.

Calibration and bench checks

Before the booth (once)

  1. Check every wire in 30 seconds — python3 ~/aquasentinel/tools/check_hardware.py prints OK / FAIL for every sensor, the beacon, the pump and the SIM800L.
  2. Calibrate pH with buffer solutions (until then the pH tile says calibrate and only a sudden pH change counts as an event): 
    sudo systemctl stop aquasentinel
python3 ~/aquasentinel/tools/calibrate_ph.py
sudo systemctl start aquasentinel
  3. One-sensor tests — python3 PCB_SENSOR_HUB_v1/firmware/wiring_test.py <test> with nano temp dht ldr led pump ph tds turb mq3 sim. Remember: MQ-3 needs ≈ 3 min warm-up; block the turbidity slot with paper to see its output fall; add salt to see TDS rise.
  4. Decision engine self-test — python3 ~/aquasentinel/tools/self_test.py (install.sh already ran it; it ends ALL PASS).
  5. TDS factor (optional) — against a trusted TDS pen; the Pi applies the SEN0244 formula with the water temperature.

Every day at the booth

  • Power up: AUX charger → J12 first, then the Pi (the Nano comes up over USB).
  • Wait for the dashboard: first 20 s LEARNING, then NORMAL (green beacon).
  • Keep the probes wet between demos; dry the MQ-3 station between solvent runs.
pH electrode care: never let it dry out — store in KCl storage solution; rinse with distilled water between waters; recalibrate if the reading drifts.

Back in clean water it returns to NORMAL after a few seconds. To start fresh with new water press Learn new water. Only one SMS is sent per 10 minutes.

How the Pi decides

  • Hard limits: pH < 6 or > 9 · dissolved solids > 1400 ppm.
  • Learned baseline: a sudden jump counts even below the limit (pH drop/rise of 1.0, TDS ×1.5 and +150 ppm).
  • Confirmation: 2 unusual readings out of the last 3 — no false alarm from one bad reading.
  • Classes: NORMAL · ACIDIC · INDUSTRIAL · RUNOFF · SOLVENT · SENSOR FAULT (a broken or missing sensor is reported as SENSOR FAULT, never as normal).
  • The model reads once a second for the demo (the river design reads every 15 minutes).

Team & project

Team Sanjivani Hero's Journey (AG26-1500$41), Sanjivani University, Kopargaon · AAKRUTI 2026 · Robotics · India Geo Finale: booth 19 Nov, pitch 20 Nov 2026 (Pune).

AquaSentinel is a solar-powered float on a stainless guide pole clamped to a bridge pier. Every 15 minutes it reads the river; when a spill passes it turns its light red, sends an SMS and keeps a bottle of that water for the lab. The 1:5 LIVE model runs the same sensors and the same decision rules on the booth table, once a second.

This build in five lines

  1. 7 sensors: PH-4502C (pH) · SEN0244 (TDS) · SEN0189 (turbidity) · DS18B20 · DHT11 · LDR · MQ-3.
  2. Arduino Nano on the Sensor Hub PCB v1.2 reads them all and drives the beacon + pump.
  3. Raspberry Pi 3B+ decides, dashboards (:8080), logs CSV, sends SMS (SIM800L).
  4. Power: AUX 5 V → J12 → step-up 9 V (pH) → step-down 4.0 V (SIM800L).
  5. Bench-tested 12–13 Nov 2026: all seven sensors, the beacon and the pump answered through the board.
