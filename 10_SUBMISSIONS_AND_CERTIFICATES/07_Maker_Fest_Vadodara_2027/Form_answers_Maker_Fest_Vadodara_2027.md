**Project description**

2:00 a.m. A tanker empties into the river. By sunrise the water looks clean, and the next manual sample is weeks away. India has 296 polluted river stretches and locations, and most are still sampled by hand about once a month, while a spill lasts only hours.

AquaSentinel is our answer: a floating river sentinel that rides a guide pole clamped to a bridge pier. The river itself lifts it up and down (2 m on 8 rollers, no anchors, no motor). Every 15 minutes it reads pH, dissolved solids, turbidity and water temperature, plus air temperature/humidity, light and vapour. A Raspberry Pi on board decides on the spot, with no cloud: hard limits plus a learned "normal for this hour". The moment a spill passes, it turns its beacon red, sends an SMS to the water-works and bottles a sample of that exact water for the lab. It even names the spill, INDUSTRIAL, ACIDIC, RUNOFF or SOLVENT, and a broken probe says FAULT, never "normal".

At Maker Fest we will exhibit our 1:5 LIVE model: 37 3D-printed parts (17 designed new in SOLIDWORKS) built around real full-size sensors, a Raspberry Pi with a SIM800L modem, a red/green beacon and a sample pump.

Hands-on activity: visitors pour a "river" of their choice into the sensor dish (clean water, salty water, vinegar or muddy water) and watch AquaSentinel decide in seconds. The beacon turns red, the SMS arrives on a phone at the stall, and the bottle fills. Kids can unplug a probe and see it report FAULT instead of pretending the water is fine.

Behind the model: a 66-part full-size design assembled and simulated in SOLIDWORKS (14 of 14 design checks, 2 simulation studies), two assembly manuals, and a costing of about ₹48,000 per sentinel, roughly 75 sentinels for the price of one real-time monitoring station.
